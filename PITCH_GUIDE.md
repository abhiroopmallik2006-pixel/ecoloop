# EcoLoop AI — Green Startup Challenge 2026

## The idea in one sentence

EcoLoop helps campuses measure resource waste, prevent avoidable surplus and track reuse through one shared workspace.

Start with cafeteria food waste as the first pilot. Water, materials and solar monitoring are expansion modules. The dashboard uses simulated examples, not measured results from JIMS or a real customer.

## Speaking notes

### 0:00–1:00 — Problem

“Good morning everyone. We are presenting EcoLoop AI, a resource-management startup concept for campuses and residential communities. Our starting point is simple: before we can reduce waste, we need to understand where it happens and what action follows.

Consider a cafeteria that prepares more meals than students need. The leftovers are visible, but the information behind the decision may be scattered across attendance records, handwritten logs and separate teams. We propose one shared workflow that connects measurement, planning and verified follow-through.”

### 1:00–2:00 — Solution

“EcoLoop follows four steps: measure, understand, act and verify. A campus team records quantities, initially through manual entries in a future pilot. The system uses those records to suggest a practical next step. A responsible person reviews the suggestion. Finally, the team records evidence of the outcome.

Our first pilot would focus on one cafeteria. We would expand into material recovery, approved water reuse and energy monitoring after validating the first workflow. This staged approach keeps the initial project achievable.”

### 2:00–4:00 — Demonstration

Open `/demo?view=food&site=campus&period=week`.

“This is our interactive prototype. All values shown here are simulated. The food graph displays recorded food waste in kilograms. It does not claim verified savings.”

Open “Prepare a little less. Serve just enough.”

“The example starts with four comparable services: 100, 110, 90 and 120 portions. Their median is 105. Adding an illustrative buffer of 15 gives a proposed plan of 120 portions, compared with the current plan of 150. That is a proposed reduction of 30 portions. A cafeteria manager must check attendance and approve any actual change. The current prototype explains a fixed example; it is not a live forecasting service.”

Open Resource passports and `ECO-0041`.

“This passport shows the intended evidence trail from measurement to dispatch and recipient confirmation. A proposed transfer is different from a completed recovery. Our goal is to make that difference visible.”

If time remains, show the blocked water recommendation: “A sensor reading alone does not establish water quality. This example blocks the route until quality evidence is reviewed.”

### 4:00–5:00 — Business model

“The proposed paying customer is the campus or society administration. Our revenue hypothesis is a recurring subscription for the shared dashboard, workflow and reporting, with an optional setup and training fee. Sensors could be an optional later integration rather than an initial requirement.

We have not validated pricing or secured customers. Our first commercial test would ask whether the operator's verified savings and reporting benefits justify the service cost.”

### 5:00–6:00 — Impact and differentiation

“We would evaluate food waste per meal served, rather than compare only total kilograms across days with different attendance. A pilot would also record portions prepared, meals served and actual operating costs.

Other modules would keep their own units: litres for water, kilowatt-hours for solar generation and kilograms for materials. We would not add unlike quantities into a misleading total. Our proposed distinction is the connection between an explainable recommendation, a responsible reviewer and evidence of what actually happened.”

### 6:00–7:00 — Roadmap and closing

“Our proposed four-week pilot begins with one cafeteria. Week one establishes the recording process and baseline. Week two collects comparable service data. Week three introduces supervised planning suggestions. Week four reviews outcomes and user feedback. Longer observation may be needed before claiming reliable impact.

The next engineering stage adds persistent records, authenticated roles and real input-driven recommendations. The current prototype demonstrates the interface and reasoning, while live integrations remain future work.

We are seeking permission to explore a small supervised pilot and feedback from cafeteria and facilities operators. EcoLoop's goal is to help a community turn better information into less waste. Thank you.”

## Three-minute Q&A preparation

**Is the AI working?** The current app shows deterministic, explainable recommendation examples. It has no live model call or trained forecasting pipeline. Future prediction must be validated against simple baselines before adoption.

**What works today?** Responsive pages, interactive dashboard filters, resource views, recommendation explanations, passport history views, GreenPoints examples and simulated CSV export.

**What does not work yet?** Real accounts, persistent operational records, live sensors, live AI inference, actual approvals/transfers and real rewards.

**Who pays?** Campus or society administration is the proposed buyer. Subscription and setup fees are hypotheses to test in a pilot.

**How much waste have you reduced?** No real-world reduction has been measured. Demo numbers are fictional; impact must be established from pilot records with appropriate comparisons.

**Why not use a spreadsheet?** A spreadsheet is a valid pilot baseline. The proposed product adds shared review states, traceable handoffs and consistent reporting; interviews should establish whether that is worth paying for.

**Is this a food-donation app?** The initial focus is prevention and resource tracking. Any future food redistribution would need an approved operational process; the demo does not certify food safety.

**Why four modules?** They demonstrate the broader platform direction. The first operational pilot is deliberately limited to one resource stream.

**How does this relate to health?** The intended relevance is cleaner shared environments and accountable handling of resources. We are not claiming clinical or measured health outcomes.
